Passports, visas, and confirmations end up in separate piles

Rick Steves's document list is short and easy to postpone. Photograph or scan the passport and any visa, keep a digital copy you can open, and leave another copy with someone at home. If you travel with a companion, carry copies of each other's passports. On the Rick Steves forum, travelers describe what they actually do: a binder of printouts, a mail folder of confirmations, a spreadsheet, a calendar, and a cloud drive. Each system works until the one file you need is in the system you cannot open. TripCheck is a single vault for those pages. Free holds two active documents. The binder, the inbox, and the drive

A passport photo lives in the camera roll. The visa file is in email. The hotel confirmation is in another thread, and the insurance certificate is a download you meant to rename. That scatter is what the forum is negotiating, one workaround at a time. Rick Steves's reason for gathering them is practical: a lost passport, a rental desk that cannot find the booking, or a phone that will not reach the cloud. A copy that exists only online fails the second of those. A copy that exists only on paper fails if the folder stays in the hotel safe. The travelers who sound prepared are the ones who picked a place and finished the job before the taxi.
