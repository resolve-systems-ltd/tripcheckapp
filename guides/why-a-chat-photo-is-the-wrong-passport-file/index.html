A passport photo in a hotel chat outlives the stay

ADAC describes a familiar check-in. A receptionist copies an identity card, a host photographs a passport, or an online booking will not continue until a scan is uploaded. The German motoring club tells guests to ask which duty requires a full copy, whether showing the document is enough, and to send a copy only by a secure route when there is no alternative. The Dutch data protection authority draws a harder line for the Netherlands: a hotel, bed and breakfast, holiday park, or campsite there is not allowed to copy, scan, or photograph your passport. Writing down some details, such as your name, can be allowed. Other countries set other rules, so a Dutch limit is not a world rule. A picture dropped into a booking chat has no expiry. The desk wants the page, and the thread keeps it

The hotel needs to register the guest. The fastest reply is the photo page in the same conversation as the booking. That image then sits on every phone in the thread, including staff who join later, and you cannot expire it when the stay ends. Ordinary email has the same long memory. ADAC's point is that a full copy can disclose more than a form needs, and that an open message is a poor way to move it. In the Netherlands the authority says the copy should not be made at all. Elsewhere you may still be asked. The useful habit is to find out whether a look at the passport, or a few typed fields, will do before you attach the whole page.
