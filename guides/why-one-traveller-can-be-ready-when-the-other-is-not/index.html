A shared booking does not clear the passenger beside you

The name on a booking is not a travel document. The Times of India reported a family who had booked a Bali package together and were stopped in Delhi because one son's passport had less than six months of validity left. The trip had been arranged for the whole group. Only one of them was refused. The Independent has reported a related pattern: airline staff sometimes apply a passport test that is stricter than the rule the destination publishes, and a correctly documented traveler can still be turned away until someone checks the real requirement. One reservation, two outcomes at the desk

In the Surat report, the passports were already with the person who arranged the trip. That did not make them equivalent. At the airport the rule being applied was about the remaining validity on one passport, and that traveler did not continue. A message thread can show who paid for the hotel and who is on the reservation. It cannot show an expiry date, and the airline asks for the passport, not the confirmation email. The Independent's reporting is a warning in the other direction as well. Staff have refused boarding on a reading that was not the published rule. You want each person's dates checked before you are standing at the counter, and you want that check set beside the official advice for the country you are entering, not beside a slogan left over from another trip.
